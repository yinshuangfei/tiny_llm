A small language model is trained on a mixture, not on one repeated paragraph. Raw documents are stored by domain: dialogue, prose, code, math, instructions, tool traces, and longer notes. Exact duplicates are removed first, because a repeated page would dominate the loss. A language label is then attached by looking at the letters. Chinese, English, and mixed sentences stay in the mixture, while empty text is dropped. A quality filter removes documents that are too short or that repeat the same word. A safety filter removes text that asks for dangerous instructions. After those filters, domain weights decide how many tokens each kind of document contributes. Dialogue receives the largest share so the model keeps seeing User and Assistant turns. Code and math receive enough share to show a second pattern, and long notes are split across packed rows instead of being cut at a random character. The tokenizer is trained on the kept documents only, not on the upsampled mixture, so repeated sampling does not invent new merge rules. Packing then concatenates documents with a blank line and cuts the token stream into fixed rows. Each row is one token longer than the training window, which lets the trainer shift it into an input and a target without reading the next row. Preference pairs are written beside this stream and are not packed into it. They stay available for a later alignment stage. This is the same order a larger training stack uses, scaled down to a corpus that still fits on one machine.
